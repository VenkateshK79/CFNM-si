What this measures

Relative search interest for the selected terms, time range and geography. A value of 100 represents the peak relative interest within the selected comparison.

What it does not measure

It does not provide a definitive count of searches, users, traffic, revenue, audience size or commercial intent.

Reading the chart responsibly

Use the same geography and time period when comparing terms. Treat isolated spikes cautiously, especially for low-volume terms. Related-search data can also be incomplete or filtered.

Search-interest methodology

When CFNM.si publishes Google Trends observations, the figures will be treated as relative search interest, not absolute search volume. Google normalizes Trends data to a 0–100 scale based on the selected time and geography.

Important: a value of 100 means the highest relative interest in the selected comparison, not 100 searches. Low-volume queries may not generate a usable Trends graph.

Data standards

  • Identify the source and date of every measurement.
  • Keep geography and comparison periods consistent.
  • Do not convert normalized Trends scores into invented search counts.
  • Separate observed data from interpretation.
  • Use public sources and avoid reproducing copyrighted adult material.
